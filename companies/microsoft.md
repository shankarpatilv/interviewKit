# Company: Microsoft

## Interview Focus

Microsoft behavioral interviews evaluate how candidates learn, collaborate,
make decisions, and connect technical work to customer impact. Strong answers
should explain what changed, what the candidate learned, how feedback shaped
the approach, and how the work helped users or partner teams.

Use a STAR(R) structure for Microsoft answers:

- **Situation**: Set the scene briefly.
- **Task**: State the candidate's specific responsibility.
- **Action**: Spend most of the answer on decisions, collaboration, tradeoffs,
  and execution.
- **Result**: Quantify the outcome where possible.
- **Reflection**: Explain what was learned and what changed afterward.

## Principles Or Competencies

### Collaboration

Communicate clearly within and across teams. Show how you listened, included
others, handled disagreement, and improved the team's outcome.

### Drive For Results

Deliver on important goals with accountability and persistence. Show how you
prioritized, handled setbacks, and measured progress.

### Customer Focus

Connect technical decisions to customer or partner impact. Strong answers tie
architecture, reliability, cost, quality, or delivery tradeoffs back to user
value.

### Influencing For Impact

Persuade through context, data, and clear communication. Show how you aligned
stakeholders, changed a decision, or helped others commit to a direction.

### Judgment

Make sound decisions in complex situations. Show how you scoped the problem,
used evidence, weighed tradeoffs, and adapted when assumptions changed.

### Adaptability

Handle ambiguity and change with curiosity and resilience. Show how you learned
from feedback, recovered from mistakes, and adjusted the plan.

## Culture Signals

Microsoft also emphasizes growth mindset, customer obsession, inclusion, and
cross-company collaboration. Use these themes to choose stories with learning,
empathy, ownership, and customer impact.

## Notes

Common Microsoft behavioral themes include collaboration, ambiguity, growth
mindset, customer impact, influence, judgment, and learning from feedback.
Avoid repeating the same story across a loop when possible, and prepare
follow-up details about tradeoffs, metrics, lessons learned, and what would be
done differently.
